囲碁 · igo · Japan

A friendly club for an ancient game

Go (also known as baduk, or weiqi) is one of the oldest board games still played today. The game is simple to learn, but deep enough to spend a lifetime on. It's also just a good excuse to sit across a table from someone for an evening.

"Go" is the Japanese name. In Korea it's baduk (바둑); in China, where the game began some four thousand years ago, it's weiqi (围棋). Same stones, same board, same quiet concentration.

The Duluth Baduk Club is casual and welcoming. Everyone is welcome, from "I've never placed a stone" to dan-level players. If you're new, someone will teach you and play you on a handicap, beginners are happily welcome. Come every time or once a season; both are completely normal.

바둑 · baduk · Korea

When we meet

First and third Wednesday of every month, 6:30 PM until the cafe closes, at The Loch. Just walk in — there's no sign-up.

Next meetup: the next first or third Wednesday

What a night looks like

  • Show up, grab a board, get paired with someone near your strength.
  • Usually two or three games over the evening, with time to watch and talk between.
  • New players start with a short teaching game on a small board.
  • The cafe has food, coffee, and a wall of other games if you need a break.

围棋 · wéiqí · China

New to Go? Start here.

You can learn enough to play a real game in about ten minutes. You don't have to study anything first — but if you like to read ahead, these are good:

  • The Interactive Way to Go — click-through lessons, no account needed
  • Learn to Play Go (OGS) — a short interactive tutorial
  • Learn to Play (American Go Association) — rules, videos, and a beginner's guide
  • Sensei's Library: the rules of Go — the deep end, when you're curious

Or skip all of it and just show up — teaching people is half the fun.
